`gmail.draft_compose`, `gmail.draft_update`, and `gmail.draft_reply` take optional `attachments`: an array of `{ filename, mimeType, contentBase64 }` (new exports `GmailDraftAttachment`, `GmailDraftAttachments`, `GmailDraftAttachmentFilename`, `GmailDraftAttachmentMimeType`, `GmailDraftAttachmentBase64`), matching the `filename`/`mimeType` `gmail.list_attachments` reports and the standard-base64 `contentBase64` `gmail.get_attachment` returns. At most 10 attachments (`gmailDraftAttachmentsMaxItems`) and 25 MiB decoded in total (`gmailDraftAttachmentsMaxBytes`); filenames without control characters, path separators, or bidirectional controls; parameterless `type/subtype` media types other than `multipart/*` and `message/*`; canonical padded base64. With attachments the draft is 7-bit `multipart/mixed` (the unchanged body first, then base64 attachment parts with RFC 2047 `name` and RFC 2231 `filename*`) written with one never-retried simple multipart media upload to `upload/gmail/v1/users/me/drafts?uploadType=multipart` (`PUT .../drafts/{id}?uploadType=multipart` for updates); headers, send-as checks, and reply derivation are unchanged. Host HTTP adapters must forward the `multipart/related` body exactly and accept outgoing bodies up to about 35 MiB. Decoded MIME over 35 MiB (`gmailDraftMessageMaxBytes`) fails before credentials or any request with `validation_failed` and `underlying: { outcome: 'rejected', retryable: false, reason: 'too_large' }`. Drafts without attachments (absent or empty) are written exactly as before through the JSON endpoint. `gmail.draft_update` replaces the whole message, so attachments not sent again are removed.
